🎯 Showcase Rubric • Project Key Message / Thesis Statement (2–3 Sentences)

ReturNTU addresses the systemic failure of voluntary BYO campaigns across NTU by replacing individual remembrance with an on-demand, zero-cost reusable container borrowing loop integrated into student passes. By routing containers through existing canteen dishwashing lines and capacity-monitored return kiosks, the system eliminates food-soiled packaging waste at the source and reaches environmental break-even within 13 cycles.

Prior Intervention Benchmark: EarthLink NTU BYO Trial

Jan–Feb 2026 Trial Study

EarthLink NTU ran a 1-month Bring Your Own Container campaign offering 10¢–30¢ off across 5 stalls in 3 canteens. Despite cash incentives, voluntary BYO faces an insurmountable barrier: forgetting.

✕ The BYO Memory Barrier (Zero Waste SG / Eco-Business)
  • • Forgetting is universal: Remembering to pack a container before morning classes is the single most cited barrier to reuse schemes.
  • • Backpack soiling: Students resist carrying greasy chili-oil containers inside their bags throughout afternoon lectures.
  • • Insufficient incentive: A 10¢–30¢ discount does not overcome the friction of washing in communal hall sinks.
✓ The ReturNTU Systemic Solution
  • • Zero memory burden: Ask for a reusable container at checkout at no extra cost. QR codes track usage automatically.
  • • No student washing: Drop soiled bowls at convenient stations equipped with capacity sensors; sent to canteen wash facilities.
  • • Closed-loop responsibility: Return within 7 days to earn canteen discount points; late fees prevent inventory shrinkage.
Systems Analysis

Trade-offs, physics, and equity.

A circular model cannot simply assume good intentions. It must account for energy balances, hawker costs, and socioeconomic realities.

01 / Environment

Break-Even & Washing Mileage

Reusables start with a higher manufacturing footprint. Nordic studies show bowls require roughly 13 cycles to break even with single-use plastics (6 for cups).

Identified Risk: Energy wasted if washing stations are far and require vehicle haulage.
→ Mitigation: Canteen-level localized washing via pushcarts.
02 / Economics

Hawker & University Outlays

Two primary economic beneficiaries: stall owners spend less recurring cash on disposable packaging, and NTU spends less on municipal waste disposal hauling.

Identified Risk: Hawker resistance during fast lunch rush.
→ Mitigation: Free borrow at checkout, agreed pricing structures fixed before pilot deployment.
03 / Society

Hygiene Psychology & Equity

Aligns with NTU's sustainability vision, but faces student psychological hesitation regarding communal hygiene, plus fear of deposits on a tight budget.

Identified Risk: Regressive fees for needy students.
→ Mitigation: Fee waivers for financial aid students, digital refunds, SFA commercial sanitation.

Who Acts & Main Risk Mitigations (From Document)

Stakeholder Governance
  • NTU Administration: Approves policy, funds pilot drop stations, and absorbs initial container pool.
  • Participating Stalls: Hand out reusables as default option at checkout.
  • Canteen Cleaners: Wash containers at existing central dishwashing facilities.
  • EarthLink NTU: Leads student promotion and peer onboarding campaigns.
  • SFA: Ensures compliance with national food hygiene rules and sanitization temperatures.
Main Risks & Concrete Fixes
  • Risk: Low uptake / opt-out habit.
    → Fix: Make reusables the default option at checkout; student must explicitly request single-use.
  • Risk: Lost / unreturned containers.
    → Fix: QR code tracking linked to NTU matric pass, with a small late replacement fee after 7 days.
  • Risk: Lack of stallholder buy-in.
    → Fix: Agreeing on fixed packaging unit costs before the pilot, guaranteeing savings over disposable boxes.
